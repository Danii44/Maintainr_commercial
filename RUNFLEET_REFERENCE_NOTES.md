# RunFleet Reference Notes

> These notes identify public information-architecture patterns only. Maintainr will not copy RunFleet branding, layouts, wording, testimonials, customer claims, or visual assets.

## Public Patterns Observed

RunFleet positions itself around a single operational promise, then gives visitors three recurring routes: an interactive demo, a feature overview, and a sales conversation. Its public structure progresses from the operational problem, through role- and industry-relevant capability groups, to trust and reporting explanations, before returning to a demo or consultation conversion point. The feature page uses focused sections for work orders, assets, audit history, mobile work, and reporting rather than overwhelming visitors with a product map.

Its public demo landing asks for a small set of qualification inputs before offering a tailored workspace preview: a work email, property category, portfolio-size range, and optional areas of interest. Maintainr can adapt the low-friction sequencing, but must keep the current **browser-only fictional demo** and avoid collecting unnecessary data before visitors can explore it.

## Maintainr Adaptation

Maintainr should retain a distinct property-maintenance focus: resident reporting, manager coordination, technician completion proof, owner visibility, bilingual English/Arabic access, and private multi-workspace boundaries. The refreshed commercial experience should put **Explore demo**, **Request a demo**, and **Request a quotation** ahead of an existing-customer sign-in link. The live SaaS should remain a protected workspace destination; the public demo must continue to use fictional browser-only data and never access customer records.
